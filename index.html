About the Project

Layer 1

Main Content

This project investigates microorganisms found in pond water. It combines visual observation, bacterial isolation, staining methods, and DNA analysis to better understand how bacteria grow and how different laboratory methods help identify them.

Project Goal

Study microbial life in pond samples and organize the results into a clear visual website.

Project Focus

Observation, culturing, identification, and genetic analysis.

Quick Points

  • Pond samples were examined from multiple locations.
  • The work focused mainly on bacteria instead of only algae.
  • The final site is designed to explain the full process clearly.

Microscopy

Layer 2

Main Content

Microscopes were used to examine pond water and identify what kinds of microorganisms were present, including algae, bacteria, and other microscopic life. Samples from several ponds were compared to see how visible life differed by source.

Why Microscopy Matters

It gives the first visual look at the biodiversity inside the pond water.

Quick Points

  • Pond 1, 2, and 3 were observed.
  • Some days also included pond 0.
  • Bacteria were hard to track because they were tiny and moved fast.

Bacteria Culturing

Layer 3

Main Content

Bacteria culturing began on April 2. The purpose was to isolate bacteria from pond water and grow colonies that could be studied more easily than free-moving bacteria under a microscope. Agar plates and blood plates were both used.

Initial Expectation

Higher temperatures around 30°C were expected to support faster bacterial growth.

Follow-Up Plan

Use different temperatures such as 32°C and 28°C to compare bacterial growth patterns.

Quick Points

  • Agar plates and blood plates were both tested.
  • Bacteria growth on blood plates raised questions.
  • The goal was to isolate single bacteria instead of mixed colonies.

Gram Staining

Layer 4

Main Content

Gram staining was scheduled as part of the next phase of the experiment. This method uses multiple dyes to help distinguish bacteria based on cell wall structure, helping classify them as gram-positive or gram-negative.

Why It Helps

It provides a clearer way to identify bacteria after culturing them on plates.

Quick Points

  • Crystal violet, iodine, alcohol, and safranin are used in sequence.
  • The result helps separate bacterial groups visually.

DNA Isolation

Layer 5

Main Content

DNA isolation was used to extract genetic material from bacterial cultures. This stage moved the project from visible observation into molecular analysis.

Key Steps

Lysis, precipitation, and purification.

Purpose

Prepare bacterial DNA for later visualization and analysis.

Quick Points

  • DNA extraction adds a deeper scientific layer to the project.
  • It supports more advanced biological analysis beyond microscopy.

Agarose Gel Electrophoresis

Layer 6

Main Content

Agarose gel electrophoresis is a molecular biology technique used to separate and visualize DNA based on size and charge. DNA moves through an agarose matrix under an electric field, with smaller fragments moving faster.

Materials

Agarose gel, ionic buffer, electric current, and fluorescent dyes.

Applications

DNA analysis, cloning, PCR verification, and fragment separation.

Quick Points

  • DNA is negatively charged because of its phosphate backbone.
  • Fragments travel toward the positive electrode.
  • Smaller fragments move more easily through the gel pores.
